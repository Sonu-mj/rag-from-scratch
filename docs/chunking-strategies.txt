Chunking Strategies for RAG

Chunking decides how documents are sliced before embedding, and it has a
big effect on retrieval quality. The simplest approach is fixed-size
chunking: cut the text every N characters with some overlap. It is easy to
implement but can split sentences in awkward places.

A better approach is sentence-aware chunking: still target roughly N
characters, but move the cut point to the nearest sentence boundary. This
keeps each chunk readable and self-contained, which helps both the
embedding model and the final answer.

Overlap matters too. With zero overlap, an idea that straddles a boundary
may be split across two chunks and match neither well. An overlap of 10 to
20 percent of the chunk size is a common starting point.

For structured documents, consider semantic chunking: split on headings or
paragraphs instead of character counts. Tables and code blocks should
usually be kept whole, since splitting them destroys their meaning.
